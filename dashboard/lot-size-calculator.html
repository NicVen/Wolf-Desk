How the lot size is worked out

Three numbers decide it: how much you are willing to lose, how far away your stop is, and how much one point of movement is worth on 1.00 lot.

Lot size = money at risk ÷ (stop distance × value of one point per lot)

  • Gold (XAUUSD): 1.00 lot is usually 100 ounces, so a $1 move is $100 per lot.
  • Silver (XAGUSD): 1.00 lot is usually 5,000 ounces, so a $0.01 move is $50 per lot.
  • EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD: one pip on 1.00 lot is $10.
  • USDJPY, USDCAD, USDCHF: one pip is worth 10 units of the second currency, so it changes with the price. That is why the calculator asks for the current price.

Worked example: gold

Your account is $10,000 and you risk 1%, so $100. Your stop is $8.50 below your entry. One lot loses $8.50 × 100 = $850 if the stop is hit. $100 ÷ $850 = 0.117, rounded down to 0.11 lots, which risks $93.50.

Questions

Why round down?

Rounding down to the nearest 0.01 lot means the trade never risks more than the limit you set.

What if the answer says "too wide"?

Even the smallest lot (0.01) would lose more than your limit. Either the stop is too far away for this account size, or the trade is too big for your rules.

What risk per trade do traders use?

Many traders, and most prop-firm challenge rules, keep it around 0.5% to 1% per trade so a losing streak does not empty the account. The figure is your call.

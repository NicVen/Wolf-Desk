How margin is worked out

Margin = contract size × lots × price in dollars ÷ leverage.

  • EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD, NZDUSD: 100,000 units × the price. At 1.1000 that's $110,000 per lot, so at 1:100 the margin is $1,100.
  • USDJPY, USDCAD, USDCHF: the first currency is already dollars, so 1 lot is $100,000 whatever the price. At 1:100 that's $1,000.
  • Gold (XAUUSD): 100 ounces per lot. At $2,400 that's $240,000 per lot, so at 1:100 the margin is $2,400.
  • Silver (XAGUSD): 5,000 ounces per lot.

Worked example: gold at 1:30

You open 0.20 lots of gold at $2,400 with 1:30 leverage. 100 × 0.20 × 2,400 = $48,000 of gold. $48,000 ÷ 30 = $1,600 margin. On a $10,000 account that ties up 16% of it. With 1:100 the same trade needs only $480, but the loss if gold moves against you is exactly the same.

Margin is not your risk

Higher leverage lowers the margin, not the loss. What you can lose is set by your stop and your lot size. The lot size calculator works that out.
